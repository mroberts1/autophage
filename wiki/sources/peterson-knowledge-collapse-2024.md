## Abstract

> The paper examines how widespread AI adoption may unexpectedly damage public understanding despite AI's capacity to process vast data. Large language models naturally generate outputs toward the distribution's center, which proves useful generally. However, "recursive AI systems could lead to a process we define as 'knowledge collapse'" that may harm innovation and cultural richness. The research models how learners choose between traditional methods and discounted AI assistance, finding that a 20% cost reduction produces beliefs 2.3 times further from truth compared to no discount scenarios. The author provides empirical approaches to measuring LLM output distribution diversity and discusses research directions to prevent such outcomes.

## Notes

Introduces the term [[knowledge-collapse]] for the epistemic version of model collapse: as society relies on AI for knowledge generation, the center-seeking tendency of LLMs erodes the diversity and accuracy of collective understanding. The economic framing (learners choosing between AI and traditional methods based on cost) is distinctive. Key bridge between technical collapse and cultural/epistemic collapse.
