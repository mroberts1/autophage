## Abstract

> The study examines what happens when language models train on artificial data created by earlier versions of themselves. Rather than focusing on standard performance measures, researchers assessed how this recursive training affects linguistic variety across lexical, syntactic, and semantic dimensions. They conducted experiments using different natural language generation tasks in English. The results demonstrate that "a consistent decrease in the diversity of the model outputs through successive iterations" occurs, with particularly noticeable effects for creative tasks. The authors conclude that training on synthetic text poses meaningful risks to preserving the linguistic richness of language models.
