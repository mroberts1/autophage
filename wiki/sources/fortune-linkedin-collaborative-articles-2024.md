## Summary

Investigative piece on LinkedIn's "Collaborative Articles" feature (launched March 2023) as a live case study in AI content feedback loops. LinkedIn uses AI to generate questions, users answer them, LinkedIn uses contributions to train future models — and many of the "user" responses appear AI-generated themselves.

Key points:
- Critics describe the articles as "laughably low-quality," "overly basic or regurgitated," and "an ever-deteriorating ouroboros of thought leadership wank and AI word vomit" (Developer Cassie Evans)
- Researcher Abhishek Gupta: "AI-generated content entering the training feedback loop reduces the performance of future generations of models"
- LinkedIn had 10 million contributions and 270% readership growth by April 2024, despite quality concerns
- The platform uses contributions to improve recommendations and search, but AI training use isn't prominently disclosed in user agreements
- Similar features spreading to Instagram, Snapchat, X — the feedback loop pattern potentially scaling across social media

The LinkedIn case is significant as one of the earliest publicly documented, large-scale real-world instances of a self-consuming AI content loop operating in a consumer product.
