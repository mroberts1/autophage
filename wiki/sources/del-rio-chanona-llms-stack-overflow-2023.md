## Abstract

> The research examines whether ChatGPT's release affected publicly generated knowledge on the web. Researchers analyzed Stack Overflow activity, comparing it to platforms with limited ChatGPT access and areas where the model performs less effectively. They found "a 16% decrease in weekly posts on Stack Overflow" relative to comparable platforms. The effect grew over time and was most pronounced for widely-used programming languages. Since newer posts maintained similar quality scores, the decline appears driven by users shifting to ChatGPT rather than duplicate content removal. The authors conclude that while LLMs improve individual problem-solving efficiency, widespread adoption may reduce future training data availability by diminishing public knowledge exchange online.

## Notes

Empirical evidence of the self-consuming loop at a societal scale: LLMs reduce the human-generated data (Stack Overflow posts) that future LLMs need to train on. The 16% reduction in posts is a concrete, measurable effect. Key evidence for [[peterson-knowledge-collapse-2024]]'s theoretical framework playing out in practice.
