# Knowledge Collapse

Knowledge collapse is the term introduced by [[peterson-knowledge-collapse-2024]] for the epistemic analog of [[model-collapse]]: as societies rely increasingly on AI for knowledge generation and retrieval, the center-seeking tendency of large language models erodes the diversity, accuracy, and richness of collective human knowledge.

## Mechanism

LLMs generate outputs that cluster near the center of their training distribution — this is an intrinsic property of how they work. Individually, this is often beneficial (common cases are handled well). But when AI outputs replace or suppress the human-generated content that future models will be trained on, rare, heterodox, and domain-specific knowledge is progressively excluded from the loop. The result: a gradual narrowing of what is collectively known and expressed.

## Empirical Evidence

- [[del-rio-chanona-llms-stack-overflow-2023]]: ChatGPT's deployment reduced Stack Overflow activity by 16%, cutting a key source of specialist human knowledge
- [[gray-chatgpt-contamination-2024]]: at least 1% of 2023 scholarly publications were LLM-assisted, a figure almost certainly growing
- [[doshi-hauser-generative-ai-creativity-2024]]: AI assistance improves individual creative output but reduces collective diversity

## Relation to Model Collapse

Knowledge collapse is model collapse generalized from the model to the society. The self-consuming loop operates at the civilizational scale: AI generates content, that content displaces human content, future AI trains on less-diverse human data, AI outputs become more homogeneous.
