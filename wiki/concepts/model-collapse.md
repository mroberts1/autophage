# Model Collapse

Model collapse is the progressive degradation of a generative model's output quality and diversity when it is trained on data generated by earlier versions of itself or other AI models. First named and systematically studied in 2023, it has become a central concern for the long-term viability of large-scale AI training as AI-generated content proliferates on the internet.

## Mechanism

The core dynamic: a model generates synthetic data, that data enters the training corpus, and the next generation of the model is trained on a mix of real and synthetic data. Over successive iterations, the model's output distribution drifts away from the true data distribution. Two failure modes emerge:

- Loss of the tails: rare, unusual, or minority cases are underrepresented and eventually forgotten
- Mode collapse: outputs converge toward a narrow, repetitive subset of the distribution

[[marchi-heat-death-generative-models-2024]] formalizes this as a dynamical systems problem, showing asymptotic collapse toward either a Dirac delta (mode collapse) or uniform distribution.

## Key Findings

- [[shumailov-curse-of-recursion-2023]] coined "model collapse" and showed tails of the distribution disappear first
- [[alemohammad-self-consuming-go-mad-2023]] distinguished quality (precision) vs. diversity (recall) degradation and introduced the term [[model-autophagy-disorder]] (MAD)
- [[guo-curious-decline-linguistic-diversity-2023]] showed linguistic diversity degrades across lexical, syntactic, and semantic dimensions
- [[bohacek-nepotistically-trained-2023]] showed collapse in image models is not easily reversed by subsequent training on real data
- [[dohmatob-tale-of-tails-2024]] connected collapse to a change in scaling laws

## Is It Inevitable?

No, under the right conditions. [[gerstgrasser-model-collapse-inevitable-2024]] shows that if real data is *accumulated* (not replaced) alongside synthetic data, collapse is averted with bounded error. The replacement vs. accumulation regime is the critical distinction.

[[feng-beyond-model-collapse-2024]] shows that verification/filtering of synthetic data before training can also prevent collapse.

## Cultural Framing

[[chiang-chatgpt-blurry-jpeg-2023]] (February 2023) anticipates the model collapse literature from a humanist direction: LLMs are lossy compression of the web, and "the more that text generated by large language models gets published on the Web, the more the Web becomes a blurrier version of itself." The "copy of a copy" argument — each JPEG resave loses information — is the same intuition as model collapse, arrived at independently before the empirical papers.
