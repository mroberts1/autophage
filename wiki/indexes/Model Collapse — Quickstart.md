# Model Collapse — Quickstart

A short on-ramp to the model collapse literature. Two articles, then the key papers.

## The phenomenon in one paragraph

When AI models are trained on data generated by earlier AI models, their output degrades: it loses quality, diversity, and accuracy over successive generations. This happens because AI-generated data is a lossy approximation of the real thing — training on it is like repeatedly photocopying a photocopy. The technical literature calls this **model collapse** or **Model Autophagy Disorder (MAD)**. It matters because the internet is already filling with AI-generated content that future models will be trained on, whether intentionally or not.

## Start here

These two pieces require no technical background and cover the full picture:

**1. [[chiang-chatgpt-blurry-jpeg-2023]]** — Ted Chiang, *The New Yorker*, February 2023

The best conceptual frame. Chiang argues that ChatGPT is a lossy compression of the web — hallucinations are compression artifacts — and that as LLM output fills the internet, "the Web becomes a blurrier version of itself." He arrives at the copy-of-a-copy problem before the empirical research did. Read this first to build the intuition.

**2. [[nytimes-ais-output-threat-to-ai-2024]]** — *New York Times* (The Upshot), August 2024

The best empirical overview. Shows what collapse actually looks like through animated visualizations (handwritten digits degrading over 30 training iterations, text becoming incoherent). Covers the main research groups, the "ways out," and the data scarcity problem. Read this second to see the research.

## The key papers

Once you have the intuition, three papers do the foundational work:

- [[shumailov-curse-of-recursion-2023]] — coined "model collapse"; showed tails of the distribution disappear first; published in *Nature* (2024)
- [[alemohammad-self-consuming-go-mad-2023]] — introduced "Model Autophagy Disorder" (MAD); distinguished quality vs. diversity degradation; published at ICLR 2024
- [[gerstgrasser-model-collapse-inevitable-2024]] — the necessary counterpoint: collapse is *not* inevitable if real data is accumulated rather than replaced

## The central tension

The pessimistic camp ([[shumailov-curse-of-recursion-2023]], [[marchi-heat-death-generative-models-2024]]) shows collapse is real and potentially irreversible. The optimistic camp ([[gerstgrasser-model-collapse-inevitable-2024]], [[feng-beyond-model-collapse-2024]]) shows it can be prevented — but only with deliberate effort to preserve and accumulate real data.

The practical implication: high-quality, human-generated data is becoming a scarce and valuable resource.

## The social dimension

Model collapse at the level of individual AI systems becomes [[knowledge-collapse]] at the societal level: as AI use reduces the human-generated content that future AI trains on, collective knowledge narrows. Evidence:

- [[del-rio-chanona-llms-stack-overflow-2023]]: Stack Overflow posts dropped 16% after ChatGPT
- [[gray-chatgpt-contamination-2024]]: over 1% of 2023 academic papers were LLM-assisted
- [[doshi-hauser-generative-ai-creativity-2024]]: AI boosts individual output but reduces collective diversity
